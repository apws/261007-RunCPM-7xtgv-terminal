= W535 backlight minimum calibration
:source-highlighter: highlight.js

Petr reports W535's first lit level is too bright with the inherited 51/255 duty
floor. Full ON seems acceptable; he does not hear the PWM-related whine observed
on W550. These are observations, not evidence that the modules share electrical
or perceptual backlight characteristics.

== Logical meaning

* Level 0: OFF.
* Level 1: the module's lowest usable visible light, to be chosen by eye.
* Levels 2..30: intermediate brightness steps defined by that HAL.
* Level 31: full ON.

Do not describe this interface as a universal 20..100 percent brightness scale.
PWM duty ratios describe the electrical command, not calibrated emitted or
perceived light. Even equal duties need not look alike on different displays.
Manual/readme and shortcut comments were corrected; shortcut bindings themselves
were not changed.

== Temporary W535-only sweep

`hardware/arduino_7xtgv/jc8048w535_backlight.h` now uses
`round(255 * level / 31)` for the 32 table entries:

[source,text]
----
level  0..15:   0   8  16  25  33  41  49  58  66  74  82  90  99 107 115 123
level 16..31: 132 140 148 156 165 173 181 189 197 206 214 222 230 239 247 255
----

This is an electrical test sweep, not the final perceptually shaped ladder.
W550 and RP2350 tables remain byte-for-byte unchanged. PWM frequency, polarity,
OFF/full-ON handling and the shared CRT ramp logic are unchanged.
The same remembered logical value now has a different W535 duty: for example
level 16 becomes 132 rather than 145. No INI editing or transfer is required.

== Human test after requested rebuild/reflash

. Use the existing backlight chord (Ctrl+Shift+PgDn or Alt+Shift+PgDn) to reach
  the first lit level. Holding down stops at level 1, not OFF.
. From that bottom, increase one step at a time using the corresponding PgUp
  chord. Report the first comfortably usable level; one upward tap means level 2.
. If level 1 is still too bright, report that and try a later finer 1..8 duty
  sweep. If it is too dim, increase until text is comfortably visible.
. We can then set that chosen electrical minimum as the final logical level 1
  and distribute the remaining lit steps up to full ON, with further tuning
  if useful. Do not automatically choose it without Petr's visual judgment.

Brightness changes still use the existing delayed save of the global logical
level. Moving the card carries that number, not a measured brightness value.

== Minimum confirmed; first perceptual curve trial

Petr confirms duty 1 remains visible and usable in total darkness, for white paper
and current green/amber CRT palettes. With the mostly linear electrical sweep,
levels 1..8 brighten too quickly while 16..31 change little to his eye.

Keep minimum 1 and full ON 255. The new W535-only trial table uses the following
offline formula for lit levels `L=1..31`:

[source,text]
----
duty = max(L, 1 + round(254 * ((L - 1) / 30)^2.2))

level  0..15:   0   1   2   3   4   5   6   8  11  15  19  24  29  35  41  48
level 16..31:  56  65  74  84  94 105 117 129 143 156 171 186 202 219 237 255
----

This power curve spends more steps at low duty and takes wider steps at high
duty. Gamma 2.2 is an initial tuning choice, not a measurement of this module's
brightness response. The minimum `L` floor avoids duplicate rounded duties and
ineffective keypresses near the bottom. Eight-bit PWM still limits low-end
resolution; we are not changing PWM frequency or resolution here.

[cols="1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Logical level |Previous duty |New trial duty
|1 |1 |1
|2 |16 |2
|4 |33 |4
|8 |66 |11
|16 |132 |56
|24 |197 |143
|31 |255 |255
|===

The remembered logical level is unchanged, so intermediate settings will initially
look dimmer after this update. Existing ramp/shortcut logic automatically uses the
new table. W550 and RP2350 tables remain unchanged, as do INI syntax and persistence.
Six HAL/polarity host test variants pass, including the formula, strict monotonicity
and ramp/cancellation checks. No automatic rebuild or flash: source awaits the
next requested build. Petr should compare single steps over the low, middle and
high range after deployment; final perceptual acceptance remains open.
